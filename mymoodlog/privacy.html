Privacy policy

Effective 13 September 2026.

MyMoodLog is a diary. What you write in it is yours, and the short version of this policy is: it stays on your phone.

What we collect

Nothing. MyMoodLog has no account, no sign-up, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting service and no third-party SDKs. We, the people who make MyMoodLog, never receive your entries, your settings, or any identifier for you or your device.

Where your data lives

Everything you log — moods, notes, sleep, work, food, sport, meds, cycle data, plans, finances, photos — is stored in the app's own storage on your iPhone. The Apple Watch app sends your mood check-ins to the phone over the direct watch-to-phone link; nothing goes through a server. The Lock Screen widget reads today's mood from a storage area shared between the app and the widget on the same phone.

If you use iCloud Backup or an encrypted iTunes/Finder backup, MyMoodLog's data is included in that backup like any other app's. That backup is covered by Apple's privacy policy, not ours.

Backups you make

The app can export a backup file. It is a plain file that you choose where to save — in Files, in your iCloud Drive, or wherever you send it. That file contains everything in the app, including progress photos. Guard it like you would the app itself.

Apple Health

With your permission, MyMoodLog reads your daily step count from Apple Health to show it next to your day. It reads nothing else, writes nothing to Health, and the step counts stay on the phone like the rest. You can revoke this at any time under Settings › Health › Data Access & Devices.

Camera

With your permission, the camera is used for two things: a picture of the day, and scanning barcodes on food and medicine packaging. Photos are stored inside the app on your phone only.

Data shared with others: Open Food Facts

This is the one place where something leaves the phone. When you tap the button to search Open Food Facts for a food, or scan a food barcode, the app sends that search text or barcode to the Open Food Facts servers (openfoodfacts.org) to look up the product. That request carries the search text or the barcode and your device's IP address, as any web request does — and nothing else: not your diary, not what you logged, not who you are. Nothing is sent unless you tap that button or scan. Open Food Facts is a non-profit; their privacy policy is at world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy. Searching the bundled everyday-foods list and your own shelf never touches the network.

No other party receives anything from MyMoodLog.

Safety card

Under Settings › Safety you can store a helpline, one person's number and a message for them, and your therapist's number. They are shown on a card the moment you log the feeling "SOS". The numbers stay in the app's settings on your phone. The card never places a call or sends a message by itself: each button opens the Phone or Messages app, and you decide there. If you pick the person from Contacts, the app uses Apple's own picker, which hands the app only the one number you tap — MyMoodLog never reads your address book.

Notifications

Check-in, medication and task reminders are local notifications scheduled on the phone. No push service is involved.

Children

MyMoodLog is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone — see "What we collect".

Deleting your data

Delete the app and its data is gone from the phone. Backups you exported yourself are yours to delete where you saved them.

Changes

If MyMoodLog ever gains a feature that moves data off the phone, this policy will say so before that feature ships, with the date at the top updated.
